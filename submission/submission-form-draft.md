## Project

- **Project Name:** Market Integrity Desk
- **One-line Project Summary:** A pre-trade thesis stress test for Bitget rTokens: it tells a human trader whether an opportunity is ready for review, needs investigation, must wait for confirmation, or has no measured event to support it — with every call bound to inspectable evidence.

## Project Description

### Part 1 · Thesis

Bitget rTokens can trade around the clock while their U.S.-equity underlyings, reference feeds, and corporate-information timelines do not share the same availability. The resulting problem is not simply whether two prices differ; it is whether the current market state is supported by fresh and internally consistent evidence. Existing dashboards show prices, and generic chatbots explain moves, but neither makes missing evidence explicit or lets a researcher reproduce each claim.

Market Integrity Desk produces a Market State Passport for each supported rToken. Deterministic code calculates token-versus-underlying premium, quote freshness, and session consistency using declared thresholds. When the token reprices, the desk measures the move, locates where it began, retrieves timestamped headlines, and ranks each one by publication time against that boundary — labelling a headline published after the move as `TIMING_INCONSISTENT` rather than a cause. The model is a bounded investigator: it receives only recorded checks, computed metrics and source items, narrates what they support, cites supplied evidence IDs, and must abstain when facts are unavailable. The product never converts a failed request into a negative fact, predicts returns, or executes an order.

The desk also gates itself before a human acts. A single sweep applies ordered deterministic rules to the whole watchlist and returns one of four decisions per instrument — clear, investigate, wait or blocked — each naming the evidence it cites, the conditions that would change it, and the next research step. A desk is only as clear as its least verifiable instrument, so the sweep headline is the worst verdict rather than an average, and the gate records its own refusals in an audit trail with the refusal rate. Every verdict is checked against the evidence the sweep actually holds, so a verdict cannot cite a source that was never retrieved.

Finally, because "the reference cannot confirm this" is useless to a human without the obvious follow-up, the desk answers it. The stress test builds closed-market episodes from hourly rToken candles, measures the distribution of drift, and reports what the following session actually did across comparable episodes — using Yahoo-reported underlying closes where they were retrieved and labelling the proxy where they were not. It refuses to answer when there is nothing to test, and it does not count a flat close as a confirmation: a 108 bps drift that resolves to 5 bps is reported as closed flat and excluded from the rate, because the move the drift implied never happened.

The product thesis is therefore a decision stress test, not another analysis feed. A unified Decision Memo converts the current evidence into a bounded research disposition: ready for human review, investigate unresolved evidence, wait for a missing or inconsistent reference, or reject the current catalyst thesis because there is no measured repricing event. AI explains the move, while the integrity layer exposes the timestamped records behind its citations and names missing evidence. The gate verifies that inline IDs resolve to server-held evidence; it does not automatically prove that every sentence is entailed by that evidence, so the trader can inspect each claim.

A Thesis Checkpoint closes the loop for a human researcher: after a live scan and its historical context finish, the trader writes a thesis and the condition that would change their mind, then freezes the current decision, checks and source summaries in their browser. A later live scan of the same instrument shows the before/after disposition, reference basis, price gap, changed check states and evidence content. Seconds of quote-age text advancing are not mislabeled as a changed check, and a still-loading study cannot create a false reference-basis change. The desk does not pretend to adjudicate the trader's free-text condition, monitor in the background or send alerts.

### Part 2 · Target user and product value

The primary user is a research-driven Retail or Pro Bitget trader with approximately US$10,000–US$250,000 in deployable capital, medium risk tolerance, a 3–20 name tokenized-equity watchlist, and several pre-trade research checks per week. The specific use case is deciding whether an apparent rToken move is based on fresh underlying information or sits inside a stale, contradictory, or partially unobservable market state — and, once the token has repriced, which explanation the record actually supports.

The moment that matters is the one the underlying market cannot cover: while U.S. equities are closed, the rToken still trades, and a trader (or an agent acting for one) has to decide whether a 40 bps or 400 bps gap is information or noise. This desk does not answer that question with a prediction. It answers it with the record: what was fresh, what was stale, which headline was published before the move and which was published after it, and what would have to change for the conclusion to change.

This user currently has to compare exchange quotes, underlying-market timestamps, session status, and news manually. A price terminal does not distinguish unavailable depth from thin liquidity, and a generic LLM can attach a plausible but temporally impossible news explanation. The Passport compresses that research into an auditable view while keeping every conclusion inspectable.

**Observed — independent review round and its fixes (2026-09-20, production-verified):** an adversarial review of the deployed product found eight defects, each of which was reproduced before being fixed and carries a regression test. The most serious was that the interface claimed "citations verified" while only the model's own `evidenceIds` array had been checked, so a narrative that cited nothing at all, or cited an ID the server never issued, would still have been labelled verified — the exact failure this desk exists to prevent. The citation gate now extracts the inline `[id]` markers from the prose a reader actually sees and fails a narrative that cites nothing; three live production answers were replayed through the stricter check and all three passed, so the tightening rejects no valid output. Second, the stress test had a look-ahead bias: it matched episodes on the window's peak drift, which nobody watching could have known until it had already happened, and when the market was open it used the most recent episode as the example while leaving that same episode inside the sample it was compared against, so it matched itself at zero distance. Matching now keys on the drift actually observed at a stage of the window, and the example episode is excluded from its own pool; on the live desk a matched row now reads observed −30 bps at 16h in against a window peak of +52 bps, showing the two are no longer the same number. Third, the session classifier knew only the time of day and the weekend, so it labelled Christmas morning a regular session and would have demanded a live quote from a shut market while treating the available Yahoo-reported close as stale; it now computes the NYSE calendar — full holidays and half days — from the published rules, including the observation shift that closes the market on Friday 2026-07-03 because Independence Day falls on a Saturday. ### Part 4 · Progress

Built: natural-language research intake, question-aware Qwen answers, responsive Live Desk, live-session watchlist, deterministic Passport engine, explicit failure states, actionable research handoff, expandable checks, evidence timeline, per-item provenance, an observed 16-case Replay benchmark, Methodology view, Vercel scan/capture endpoints, and a bounded Qwen investigator with citation-ID validation. The current version adds a move-explanation desk: repricing detection across 5/15/60-minute windows with start location, session-aware reference selection, drift widening/convergence over 15 minutes, turnover acceleration, top-of-book spread and size, relevance-filtered timestamped headlines, and deterministic verdict buckets (`MOST_SUPPORTED`, `POSSIBLE_CONTRIBUTING`, `TIMING_INCONSISTENT`, `NO_STRONG_CATALYST`, `NO_MATERIAL_MOVE`) with an explicit confidence rule and a "what would change this conclusion" list.

It also adds a pre-trade integrity gate and a historical stress test, which together turn the desk from something that explains a move into something that decides whether the state can be trusted at all and answers the follow-up a human would ask. The gate applies ordered deterministic rules to the whole watchlist in one sweep, returns one of four decisions per instrument with cited evidence, change conditions and a next research step, takes the worst verdict as the sweep headline, self-checks that no verdict cites evidence the sweep did not retrieve, and records its own refusals in an audit trail with the refusal rate. The stress test builds closed-market episodes from hourly candles, measures the drift distribution, and matches same-direction comparable episodes to what the following session actually did — using Yahoo-reported underlying closes where retrieved and labelling the proxy where not, refusing to answer when the target is not a material move, and reporting a flat close separately instead of counting it as a confirmation. The reference layer is now two-tier: a fresh authenticated same-session quote while the underlying can trade, and the Yahoo-reported prior close while it cannot, with the tier named in every verdict. That second tier is why the desk's central case — an overnight drift nothing can confirm — works without depending on a credential, and why a closed-market gap is reported as drift rather than as an alignment break.

A production-proof strip names each live layer and links judges directly to validation, benchmark, and source artifacts. The Bitget integration targets UTA v3 market tickers/candles and Stock+ quotes/candles; the investigator uses Qwen 3.8 Max through the hackathon Responses endpoint with a server-side key.

The Thesis Checkpoint adds a human-owned longitudinal workflow: a live scan can be frozen with the trader's written thesis and invalidation condition; a later same-symbol live scan compares the evidence and deterministic disposition without rewriting the saved baseline. It is versioned browser-local storage, not an account, a notification system, or a claim that the model has proved the trader right or wrong.

Trust boundary: the previous browser-supplied investigator endpoint was retired. The client now sends only a symbol and a question to a single analysis endpoint that retrieves the sources, computes the metrics, assembles the evidence set, and then lets the model narrate it. A client can no longer submit its own "evidence" and have the model endorse it, and the model cannot rank catalysts or compute timing because those verdicts are already fixed by deterministic code before it is called.

Problems solved: direct Bitget requests timed out in the initial local network, so the application fails closed into a frozen, timestamped fixture instead of presenting stale data as live. The production deployment now retrieves authenticated Stock+ quotes and shows their actual source age; a quote beyond the live freshness ceiling cannot turn a small numerical premium into a verified alignment. Watchlist prices update only from current-session live scans, and unverifiable session status receives no positive visual treatment. The desk opens immediately without spending a Qwen request or locking the research action; after submission it exposes the transition from Bitget retrieval to Qwen audit. Reality order-book access was not assumed; the product returns `NOT OBSERVABLE`. Model output is accepted only when bracketed citations resolve to IDs in the supplied evidence set.

Not yet completed: a balanced matched-source benchmark, and external user testing, which is outside this submission's validation scope. The public rToken path, authenticated Stock+ quote path, question-aware Qwen workflow, citation gate, natural-language task, integrity sweep and stress test have all been verified. On 2026-09-14, the AAPL production run returned both the rToken and Stock+ prices and calculated a deterministic +14 bps comparison; the state remained `CAUTION` because the weekend underlying quote was stale. On 2026-09-15, both official current and historical Stock+ candle routes authenticated but returned empty lists for all four supported symbols during a U.S. intraday probe, so the original frozen benchmark was not overwritten and synthetic history was not substituted.

One honest consequence of the two-tier reference is stated plainly rather than hidden: while the underlying's main session is running, the gate requires an authenticated Stock+ quote. If none is usable it returns `BLOCKED / UNVERIFIABLE_REFERENCE`; if a quote exists but exceeds the live freshness ceiling it returns `WAIT / STALE_REFERENCE_UNCONFIRMED` for a small gap, never `CLEAR`. Outside the main session the Yahoo-reported prior-close tier can provide a dated comparison baseline without a credential; that is a closed-market reference, not a live price. The official `bitget-signal` MCP was probed with the argument shapes it publishes — 24 probes, 4 returning any data, none of them about a gated instrument or its underlying — and is withheld on that evidence; the probe is committed and reproducible, so the decision can be re-run against a measured baseline rather than a guess.

Frameworks and APIs: React, TypeScript, Vite, Vitest, Vercel Functions, Bitget UTA v3 market tickers/candles, Bitget Stock+ quotes/candles, the official **`bitget-mcp-server`** at `agent.bitget.com/mcp` (four catalog entries: underlying quote, daily candles, dividend events, earnings calendar), and Qwen 3.8 Max through the Bitget hackathon endpoint. The separate `bitget-signal` Skill layer was evaluated for crypto macro/news context and is not claimed as integrated: the reproducible probe found its transport healthy and its data unusable for this product, so it is withheld from verdicts rather than rendered as an empty panel. The two are different packages and the handbook says so — `bitget-signal` is Agent Hub's crypto Skills; `bitget-mcp-server` is US stock and ETF data.

### Part 5 · Take on AI Trading

In trading systems, the LLM should not be the calculator or an ungrounded price oracle. It is most valuable where evidence is heterogeneous: extracting event facts, comparing publication time with price-movement time, exposing contradictions, and explaining why the system must abstain. Deterministic software should own arithmetic and hard risk boundaries; the model should add evidence synthesis and workflow orchestration. Agentic trading becomes more trustworthy when the agent can prove what it knew, when it knew it, and which required facts were missing.

## Role of the LLM / AI

Qwen 3.8 Max acts as a bounded investigator and narrator. It receives the trader's exact research question together with deterministic checks, computed metrics (repricing, drift, turnover, spread, session) and recorded evidence items including each headline's publication time and timing verdict. It answers the question directly and cites only supplied source IDs. The question is treated as untrusted data and cannot override the model's evidence boundary. Qwen is prohibited from calculating premiums, drift or timing, from overturning a timing verdict, from predicting direction, from recommending a trade, from inventing missing facts, and from treating unavailable data as a negative finding. If the endpoint is unavailable, or the response lacks resolvable evidence IDs, the product keeps the deterministic narrative and labels it `RULES` rather than `QWEN`. AI coding assistance was also used during implementation, but the product's runtime AI role is the citation-preserving explanation step — the model narrates a verdict it did not produce.
